About the project

Password Hygiene and Personal Data Protection Campaign

Online accounts hold conversations, photos, schoolwork, and personal details. This campaign makes basic protection easier to understand and practise.

◎

Background

People use online services every day, but may reuse passwords, trust fake messages, or share more information than they intend. Clear guidance can help people make safer choices.

✦

Objectives

  • Explain everyday online risks in simple language.
  • Encourage strong, unique passwords and MFA.
  • Promote careful handling of personal information.
  • Invite the community to reflect through a survey.
♧

Who can use it?

College students, families, and community members who want practical starting points for protecting their accounts and personal data.

↗

Community benefit

Visitors can take away small actions to try, discuss them with others, and use the survey to help identify topics that may need more awareness.

Know what to look for

Common cybersecurity risks

Scams often rely on familiar routines and quick decisions. Pause, check, and use a trusted route to verify.

01

Weak or reused passwords

A short password or one used on many sites can put several accounts at risk if one site is breached.

Example: The same password is used for email and a shopping account.

Try this: Use a long, different password for every important account.

02

Phishing emails and fake links

A fake message tries to make you click a link or open a file by pretending to be a trusted organisation.

Example: A message says a parcel is delayed and asks you to sign in through its link.

Try this: Visit the official app or website yourself instead of using the message link.

03

OTP and password scams

A caller or message may pressure you to reveal a one-time passcode (OTP), password, or recovery code.

Example: Someone claiming to be support asks you to read out the OTP sent to your phone.

Try this: Never share these codes. Real support should not need you to tell them your OTP.

04

Personal data exposure

Information such as your phone number, address, or ID image can be misused if it reaches the wrong person.

Example: An identity document is uploaded to an unknown site to claim a prize.

Try this: Check who is asking and why before sharing; use official services only.

05

Social media privacy risks

Public posts can reveal details about your routines, family, location, or personal life.

Example: A live holiday post shows that your home may be empty.

Try this: Review your audience and location settings, and share plans thoughtfully.

06

Unauthorised account access

Someone who gets your sign-in details may read messages, impersonate you, or change account settings.

Example: A reused password is exposed on one service and tried on your email account.

Try this: Turn on MFA and secure your email account, which can help reset other accounts.
